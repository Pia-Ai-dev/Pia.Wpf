## 1. What this settles, and what it cannot

**Settles:** A2 — *route `ArtifactRef` through the existing artifact probe.* The deferral rested on a
supply figure measured over 2 events. This read measures it over a corpus roughly four times as wide,
on an unbiased prompt mix rather than the four pilot prompts.

**Cannot settle:** anything about `expectedArtifact`'s wording as a *delta*. Three comparability cuts
now separate this corpus from every earlier reading — the counts-only tally, P1, and P8 (§5). It is a
fresh baseline, not a before/after.

**Also cannot settle:** whether one machine, one provider and one afternoon generalise. They do not.
Every number below is an existence proof at n = 12 unless it says otherwise.

## 2. The pre-registered A2 decision rule

The metric is `artifactReported=True` as a share of **step outcomes** — not of runs. Twelve runs is
roughly 25–35 step outcomes, so the denominator is read off the log, never assumed.

| Reading | Decision |
|---|---|
| **≥ 40%** of step outcomes | **Build A2.** The report channel carries enough to be worth probing. |
| **≤ 12%** of step outcomes | **Drop A2**, and reopen A7's question separately. The channel is empty seven times in eight, which is what the replay reading named as the drop trigger. |
| **12% – 40%** | **A2 stays deferred.** Record the number, and name in §6 what specific observation would move it out of the band. Not "gather more data". |

The middle band is written down on purpose, because it is the likeliest landing place and the earlier
readings (12%, 25%, 29%) all sit in it. A rule with a hole where the answer probably falls is not a
rule.

**A3, A6 and A7 stay closed** until this answers. A3 depends on A2. A6/A7 were never gated on supply.

**Two secondary numbers are recorded but decide nothing here:** the collapsed distinct-artifact
file-shapedness, and whether any declaration still offers alternatives. The first is the gate-A1 metric
and has already answered (56%, gate did not close). The second answered 0-of-6 on the replay; a non-zero
here would reopen **P3**, which was decided-and-not-built on exactly that condition.

## 3. Instrument guards — read these BEFORE reading anything else

All five must be checked, and the first three must read **zero**. A non-zero value in any of them means
the numbers below are instrument error or a censored sample, not a finding.

| Grep | Must be | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `Working subpath did not resolve` | 0 | Otherwise `notFound` is the probe failing, not a miss. |
| `Artifact probe skipped` | 0 | A verify that produced no tally at all. |
| `Artifact probe failed` | 0 | Named in the brief's harvest block; the replay confirmed all three at zero. |
| `overReportCap` | 0 | Non-zero ⇒ declarations past `MaxReportedDeclarations` were never classified, so file-shapedness loses its tail. |
| `overPathCap` | 0 | Non-zero ⇒ candidates past `MaxProbedPaths` were never probed, so delivery loses its tail. |

A 12-run corpus on a six-category mix makes censoring materially likelier than the 4-run pilot did,
which is why the last two are promoted from a footnote to a guard.

Harvest discipline, from the runbook's three traps: attribute on the `[run <id>]` prefix (runs execute
concurrently), take the **last** probe line per run id (`declared` accumulates across verify passes),
then collapse replan twins by hand. Pull the lines into a scratch file every three or four runs — the
file sink rolls at 10 MB over 7 files and a Debug corpus is verbose.

### Predicted dropouts, recorded in advance

The runbook's trap 3 says a run that never reaches a plan emits no probe line, and that which prompts
drop out is provider-dependent. Predicting it beforehand is the only way a hole in the sample is
distinguishable from a hole explained away afterwards.

- **7 and 8 (D — vault)** are expected to find nothing. The throwaway profile redirects
  `AssistantFilesFolder`, and the vault is `<AssistantFilesFolder>\Vault`, so there is no vault content
  at all. Whether they *plan* or *decline* is the open question; either is a legitimate sample member.
- **4 (B — diarization)** is expected to **park as ungroundable**. It did so on both arms of the replay,
  on this provider, which the two-arm design established as a provider property rather than a P1 effect.
  It is also the run this session reuses for E9 (§5).
- **1 (A — folder inventory)** runs against an **empty** files folder. Not a wasted sample: a legitimate
  "nothing to declare" case. Read it next to the D dropouts, not as a planner property.

Everything else is expected to produce a probe line. If a category drops out that is not on this list,
that is a finding and §6 says so.

### Reported per run

`declared / fileShaped / notFileShaped / probed / found / notFound / unresolvable`, the count of
declarations still offering alternatives, and the distinct-artifact collapse. Then, over the whole
corpus: `artifactReported=` over all step outcomes.

Verified through the **UI** as well as the log, per §9 of the replay reading: the Flow rail (run
inventory and end states), the run transcript, and the Todo view. Text exposed as a UIA name is asserted
with `ww_assert_value`; rendered markdown is a `RichTextBox` and is read through `TextPattern` via
`ww_get_value`.

## 5. Protocol, and what else this one session serves

**Provider: Mistral Medium 3.5** (`mistral-medium-latest`), the profile's configured Assistant default
and the replay's provider. Fixed across the whole corpus. If it fails for planning the fallback is the
OpenRouter provider (`openai/gpt-5.6-terra`), and §6 says which one actually ran.

**Build: Debug.** `SensitiveDebug` is `[Conditional("DEBUG")]`, and the per-declaration facts block is
the only place file-shapedness and alternatives are visible at all.

**Profile:** a throwaway root, seeded per the runbook's corrected recipe — `providers.json` **and**
`settings.json`, because copying `settings.json` alone leaves the default provider id resolving to
nothing and the app falls back to an unauthenticated Pia Cloud, where every run dies on a 401.
`AssistantFilesFolder`, `DefaultWindowMode`, `AssistantAgentModeDefault` and
`AgentRunAutoApproveBuiltInWrites` are all overridden; `PIA_DATA_DIR` does not cover the first of those.

`ScheduledMaxSteps` is deliberately **left alone**. Lowering it to force a budget park would also lower
the *resume* budget — `ResumeAsync` reads the same setting for every run — so the run just re-parks. The
ungroundable park from prompt 4 is a `WaitingForInput` park through the same claim and costs nothing.

**Three other rows ride this session**, because the desktop is the scarce resource and none of them
needs a second one:

- **E7(a)** — eyeball the persona and effort pickers in the Routines editor, including the "no longer
  available" row. That row needs a job whose `PersonaId` does not resolve, which no UI can produce, so it
  is written into the copied database between two opens.
- **E7(b)** — one open against a **pre-change** profile, to exercise migration half (b), the `ALTER TABLE`
  path. Every test and every fresh profile takes `CREATE TABLE` instead. The real `history.db` is copied
  and the two `ScheduledJobs` pin columns are dropped from the copy; the same open exercises **E9's** two
  new `AgentRuns` columns for free, since the real database predates them.
- **E9** — park prompt 4, **move the per-mode persona in Settings**, then resume. Without the mid-park
  move the observation cannot tell a read-back from a re-resolution that happens to agree. The observable
  is the persona stamped on each step's persisted message (`SyncMessagePersona`, written unconditionally
  by the headless executor) plus `AgentRuns.PersonaId` read straight out of the throwaway database. The
  rigorous proof is already the unit test; this is the end-to-end confirmation.

### Three comparability cuts — what this corpus may be compared to

**Nothing.** Stated plainly so §6 cannot quietly imply a delta:

1. The counts-only tally changed what an `Artifact probe:` line carries, so the pilot's hand-counted
   57/43 is not comparable to anything after it.
2. **P1** changed the planner's wording, so file-shapedness became partly a property of the prompt.
3. **P8** changed it again, after the replay's numbers were read and written down.

The replay's post-P1 arm — `declared` 6, `fileShaped` 6, 100% collapsed — sits on the far side of cut 3.
A2's decision rule in §2 is therefore written against **absolute bands**, not against a movement.
