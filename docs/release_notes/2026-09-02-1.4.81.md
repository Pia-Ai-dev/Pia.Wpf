# Pia 1.4

## Meetings

- Drag a meeting invite onto the Meeting attendee panel — straight out of
  classic Outlook, or a saved .msg, .eml or .ics — and Pia fills in the Teams
  meeting link, picking the join link out of the organizer and dial-in links
  beside it. You still tick the confirmation and press Join meeting.

## Live transcription

- The consent sentence is shorter in all three languages, and the German one
  has lost its comma: "My name is [Name] and I accept this recording by Pia."
  The old wording invited a pause in the middle, which split it into two
  utterances and got it refused. The old wording still works.
- Pia now answers to "Pieer", the spelling the Parakeet model produces. A
  participant saying the sentence perfectly could be refused over that alone.
- Each sentence has a copy button, and the one in your language stays at the
  top of the transcription window all session. Paste it into the meeting chat:
  that is the only way somebody joining over system audio gets to read it.
- A chime confirms each accepted sentence. You hear it, the far end does not.
- A refused sentence records which of the four required parts was missing —
  the part only, never anything anyone said.

## Assistant

- Chat history is now always cleaned up, and you can keep it for up to two
  years. The "Save chat history" checkbox is gone: unchecking it never stopped
  Pia storing chats, only the cleanup, so they piled up on exactly the machines
  that had asked for the opposite. New installations keep 180 days, one that
  had the checkbox off moves to 180 rather than back to 30, and everyone else
  keeps the window they had.
- A step's file edits now collapse into one "N file(s) changed" row with the
  line totals; open it for the per-file diffs. A write still awaiting your
  approval keeps its own card.

## Vault

- The German UI says "Vault" everywhere you can save into it, with the same
  icon on every one of those buttons. It used to alternate with
  "Wissensspeicher".

## Fixes

- Starting a meeting transcription no longer raises Windows Firewall prompts.
  The browser Pia drives was asking to discover Chromecast and smart-TV
  devices, which it never needed — and on a managed PC nobody without
  administrator rights could clear that prompt.
